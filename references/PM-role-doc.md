
Project Manager (PM) / Program Manager Role Profile
1. Role Identity
Role prefix: PM
Official job titles and variants:

Project Manager (PM)
Senior Project Manager
Program Manager
Senior / Principal Program Manager
Departments / business units:

Embedded across enabling functions and business lines
Common areas include Technology Transformation, Business Enablement, People & Culture initiatives, Global Business Development, Risk Management, and Enterprise Digital Delivery
This is an enterprise-wide role family spanning both business and technology segments
Reporting lines:

Typically report to a Project or Program Management Office (PMO) or a transformation leader
In business-led programs, Program Managers may report to Director or VP-level sponsors
Seniority range / levels:

Associate / Junior Project Manager
Project Manager (mid-level)
Senior Project Manager
Program Manager
Senior / Principal Program Manager (senior individual contributor)


2. Core Responsibilities
Project scoping and planning

Define project objectives, scope, and success criteria
Gather requirements through research and stakeholder workshops
Produce project charters, work plans, milestones, and resource plans
AI opportunity: Drafting charters and work plans using clear, outcome-focused prompts
Schedule and task management

Maintain detailed work plans with tasks, owners, and deadlines
Track progress and adjust schedules as scope or dependencies change
Coordinate delivery across teams
AI opportunity: Assisting with schedule updates and draft timelines, with human verification
Risk and issue management

Maintain risk and issue logs
Assess probability, impact, and mitigation actions
Escalate major risks and drive resolution plans
AI opportunity: Pattern analysis and mitigation suggestions, validated by PM judgment
Stakeholder communication and reporting

Prepare regular status updates for teams, sponsors, and governance forums
Tailor communications for different audiences
Capture and communicate decisions and action items
AI opportunity: Drafting status reports, briefings, and meeting summaries
Project governance and compliance

Ensure adherence to the Initiative Lifecycle Framework and governance standards
Prepare documentation for stage gates and approvals
Coordinate architecture, security, and compliance reviews when required
AI opportunity: Consistency checks against templates and governance requirements
Resource and budget management

Track budgets, forecasts, and actuals
Coordinate team capacity and utilization
Manage vendor engagements in line with procurement rules
AI opportunity: Drafting budget tables and highlighting potential resource constraints
Team coordination and meetings

Plan and facilitate project meetings and workshops
Capture decisions, actions, and follow-ups
Drive execution between meetings
AI opportunity: Automated meeting summaries and action item extraction
Delivery and change implementation

Oversee delivery of project outputs
Coordinate testing, acceptance, training, and cutover
Manage project close-out and lessons learned
AI opportunity: Quality checks and acceptance criteria validation


3. Workflow and Tools
A. Workflow snapshot
Rhythm of work:

Frequent check-ins with delivery teams
Weekly status and planning activities
Periodic steering and governance reviews
Peak workload periods:

Month-end and quarter-end reporting
Stage-gate reviews and major decision points
Most time-consuming activities:

Preparing documentation and reports
Meetings, follow-ups, and action tracking
Most repetitive tasks:

Meeting notes and log updates
Chasing updates and compliance inputs
Maintaining consistency across multiple systems
B. Tools in active use

Email and collaboration: Stakeholder communication, scheduling, and updates
Team collaboration platforms: Chats, channels, file sharing, and meetings
Word processing: Charters, briefs, agendas, and minutes
Presentations: Steering updates, governance reviews, and training
Spreadsheets: Budgets, risks, issues, and trackers
Enterprise PPM tools: Lifecycle tracking, capacity management, and portfolio reporting
Agile delivery tools: Backlogs, features, and sprint tracking
Dashboards and analytics: Portfolio metrics and performance insights
Other internal systems: Service management, CRM, and approved transcription tools


4. Data and Compliance
Sensitive data handled:

Project financials and forecasts
Vendor and contract information
Internal strategies, roadmaps, and change plans
Resource allocations and delivery metrics
Key compliance constraints:

Adherence to governance frameworks and approval thresholds
Compliance with privacy, security, and procurement requirements
Alignment with information security standards for any technology change
Safe AI use boundaries:

No non-public or confidential data may be entered into unapproved AI tools
All AI-generated outputs require human review
AI use must follow approved policies and responsible use guidelines
Primary data-related risks:

Data leakage or misclassification
Incomplete records management
Bypassing required governance or reviews


5. Pain Points and AI Opportunities
Heavy manual documentation:

Significant time spent consolidating inputs and producing reports
AI opportunity: First-draft automation with human refinement
Meeting overload and note-taking:

High volume of meetings with extensive follow-up work
AI opportunity: Automated transcription and summarization
Chasing inputs and updates:

Repeated follow-ups for status, timesheets, and compliance items
AI opportunity: Proactive reminders and exception-based reporting
Cross-team dependencies:

Managing handoffs and decision bottlenecks
AI opportunity: Early identification of dependency risks
Quality and oversight pressure:

Ensuring nothing is missed across multiple workstreams
AI opportunity: Automated checks paired with PM judgment
