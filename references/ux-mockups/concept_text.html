Structuring AI Requests:
The CRAF Framework

Financial analysis write-ups are high-stakes but repetitive — making them ideal for AI assistance, provided you give the model a structured brief. The CRAF Framework turns a vague request into a controlled drafting instruction by supplying four elements every underwriting prompt needs.

C

Context

Tells the AI what case it's working on and which inputs are in scope (e.g., a pasted metrics table, deal team notes). Without context, the model invents plausible-sounding but irrelevant detail.

R

Role

Assigns a professional identity so tone and vocabulary match your credit memo. Specifying Senior Credit Underwriter shifts the output from generic finance commentary to expected risk-focused language.

A

Action

Defines the exact deliverable: a 250-word narrative, a ranked risk list, a covenant summary. Vague actions like "write the financial section" are the single biggest source of unusable AI output.

F

Format

Controls structure: headings, bullet order, word count, and section sequence. Specifying Performance / Liquidity / Leverage headings means the draft maps directly onto your memo template.

Beyond CRAF: Essential Guardrails

Effective underwriting prompts require strict boundaries to keep the draft audit-ready and prevent the model from drifting into advisory territory:

  • Use only provided data.
  • Label any missing figures as UNKNOWN.
  • Do not recommend approval or decline.
  • Strictly separate facts from interpretation.

Key Takeaway

Mastering CRAF means Copilot or any generative AI tool becomes a disciplined drafting assistant — not a free-form writer you have to heavily correct.
